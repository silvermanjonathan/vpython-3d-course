What is new today

cylinder and cone, axis, pyramid, colours as vec(r, g, b), opacity, a function that makes objects, one number that sets every size, a model kept in a list.

Deliberately re-used: Session 1: vec, pos, size, keysdown inside the loop, and a function with a parameter.

Opener · 10 minutes

Retrieval: places, the middle, and binding

Paper first.

Question: In vec(x, y, z), which number is up, and which is toward you?

y is up. z is toward you, out of the screen. x is to the right. A negative number goes the other way.

Question: A box 1 tall has its pos at y = 0, and the floor's top face is at y = 0. What does it look like, and what is the fix?

Half of the box is under the floor, because pos is the middle of the box. The fix is pos.y = 0.5, half the height, so the bottom face is on the floor.

Question: scene.bind("keydown", paint) has no brackets after paint. Why not, and who calls paint?

Brackets would call paint right now, once, with no event. Without them you are naming the function so VPython can call it later. VPython calls it every time a key goes down, and passes the event as the argument.

Chunk 1 of 6

A cylinder starts at pos and points along its axis

A cylinder is a tube. Its pos is the middle of its bottom face, not the middle of the tube, and its axis is a vector that says which way it points and how long it is. axis=vec(0, 3, 0) is a tube 3 tall pointing straight up. mag gives the length of a vector.

A cone works the same way: pos is the middle of its flat end, and axis goes from there to the point. To stack a cone on a cylinder, start the cone where the cylinder ends, at pos + axis.

from vpython import *

post = cylinder(pos=vec(0, 0, 0), axis=vec(0, 3, 0), radius=0.5, color=color.orange)
print("post starts at", post.pos)
print("post axis is", post.axis, "so its length is", mag(post.axis))
print("the top of the post is at", post.pos + post.axis)

roof = cone(pos=post.pos + post.axis, axis=vec(0, 1.5, 0), radius=0.8, color=color.red)
print("roof starts at", roof.pos, "and its point is at", roof.pos + roof.axis)

Predict: The post's axis is vec(0, 3, 0) and the roof's axis is vec(0, 1.5, 0). Where is the top of the post, and where is the point of the roof?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

post starts at <0, 0, 0>
post axis is <0, 3, 0> so its length is 3.0
the top of the post is at <0, 3, 0>
roof starts at <0, 3, 0> and its point is at <0, 4.5, 0>

The top of the post is at <0, 3, 0>: pos plus axis. The point of the roof is at <0, 4.5, 0>: the roof starts at the top of the post and its axis adds 1.5 more.

A box and a sphere put their pos in the middle. A cylinder, a cone and an arrow put it at one end. That is the one thing to remember when a shape lands somewhere you did not expect.

Chunk 2 of 6

A colour is three numbers

color.red is not a special kind of value. It is a vector: how much red, how much green, how much blue, each from 0 to 1. vec(1, 0, 0) is red. vec(1, 1, 0) is red and green mixed, which is yellow. vec(0, 0, 0) is black and vec(1, 1, 1) is white.

Because a colour is a vector, you can do arithmetic on it. Multiplying by 0.5 halves all three numbers, which makes the same colour darker.

opacity says how solid a shape looks: 1 is solid and 0 is invisible. 0.3 is glass you can see through.

from vpython import *

print("red is", color.red)
print("yellow is", color.yellow)
print("orange is", color.orange)

purple = vec(0.6, 0, 0.8)
ball = sphere(pos=vec(-2, 0, 0), color=purple)
glass = box(pos=vec(2, 0, 0), size=vec(2, 2, 2), color=color.cyan, opacity=0.3)
print("the ball's colour is", ball.color)
print("the glass box has opacity", glass.opacity)

ball.color = ball.color * 0.5
print("half as bright:", ball.color)

Predict: Yellow is red and green mixed. What three numbers does color.yellow print? And what does multiplying the purple by 0.5 do to each of its three numbers?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

red is <1, 0, 0>
yellow is <1, 1, 0>
orange is <1, 0.6, 0>
the ball's colour is <0.6, 0, 0.8>
the glass box has opacity 0.3
half as bright: <0.3, 0, 0.4>

Yellow prints as <1, 1, 0>: full red, full green, no blue. Orange is <1, 0.6, 0>, full red with some green.

Multiplying by 0.5 halves each number: <0.6, 0, 0.8> becomes <0.3, 0, 0.4>. The mix is the same, so it is still purple, and every number is smaller, so it is darker. In the tab, look through the glass box at the ball behind it.

Chunk 3 of 6

A function that makes a tree

A function can make objects. make_tree(x, z) makes a trunk and a top at the place you give it. It has no return line, because its job is what it leaves behind in the world: two new objects every time it is called.

The two parameters are the only things that change from tree to tree. Everything else, the trunk's radius, the colours, the height, is written once inside the function.

from vpython import *

ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(20, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)

def make_tree(x, z):
    trunk = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0, z), axis=vec(0, 1, 0), radius=0.2, color=vec(0.5, 0.3, 0.1))
    top = cone(pos=vec(x, 1, z), axis=vec(0, 2, 0), radius=0.8, color=color.green)
    print("tree at x =", x, " z =", z, "  the top's point is at", top.pos + top.axis)

make_tree(-4, 0)
make_tree(3, -2)
make_tree(5, 4)

Predict: The function is called three times. How many objects are in the world after the third call, counting the ground? And how high is the point of every tree?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

tree at x = -4  z = 0   the top's point is at <-4, 3, 0>
tree at x = 3  z = -2   the top's point is at <3, 3, -2>
tree at x = 5  z = 4   the top's point is at <5, 3, 4>

Seven objects: the ground, and two for each of the three trees. Every tree's point is at y = 3: the trunk is 1 tall and the top's axis adds 2, and neither number depends on x or z. The output box shows y = 3 on all three lines, with x and z matching each call.

Chunk 4 of 6

One number sets every size

A rocket is a cylinder body, a cone nose, and a fin. If every part's size is worked out from one number, height, then changing that number changes the whole rocket and the parts still fit. The body's radius is height / 6. The nose is height / 3 long. Expressions with a letter in them, like these, are what let one number drive a model.

A pyramid is the fin. Its pos is the middle of its base, like a cylinder, and its size is length along the axis, then height, then width.

from vpython import *

def make_rocket(x, height):
    body = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0, 0), axis=vec(0, height, 0), radius=height / 6, color=color.white)
    nose = cone(pos=vec(x, height, 0), axis=vec(0, height / 3, 0), radius=height / 6, color=color.red)
    fin = pyramid(pos=vec(x, height / 4, 0), size=vec(height / 4, height / 2, 0.2), axis=vec(1, 0, 0), color=color.red)
    print("rocket", height, "tall:  body radius", body.radius, "  nose tip at y =", nose.pos.y + nose.axis.y)

make_rocket(-3, 3)
make_rocket(3, 6)

Predict: The second rocket's height is twice the first. Is its body radius twice as big? Is its nose tip twice as high?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

rocket 3 tall:  body radius 0.5   nose tip at y = 4.0
rocket 6 tall:  body radius 1.0   nose tip at y = 8.0

Both double. The body radius goes from 0.5 to 1.0, because height / 6 doubles when height doubles. The nose tip goes from y = 4 to y = 8: the body's height plus a third of it, and both parts double.

The point of writing height / 6 instead of 0.5 is that the 0.5 would still be 0.5 on the bigger rocket, and the body would be a thin stick.

Chunk 5 of 6

A model kept in a list moves as one thing

Three objects make a car. Put them in a list and a loop can do the same thing to every part. Adding the same vector to every part's pos moves the whole car without changing the distances between its parts.

from vpython import *

parts = []
parts.append(box(pos=vec(0, 0.5, 0), size=vec(2, 1, 1), color=color.blue))
parts.append(sphere(pos=vec(0, 1.3, 0), radius=0.4, color=color.yellow))
parts.append(cone(pos=vec(1, 0.5, 0), axis=vec(1, 0, 0), radius=0.5, color=color.red))
print(len(parts), "parts")

for part in parts:
    part.pos = part.pos + vec(0, 0, -3)

for part in parts:
    print("part now at", part.pos)

Predict: Before the loop, the sphere is 0.8 above the box's middle. After the loop, how far above the box's middle is it, and which of the three pos numbers changed for every part?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

3 parts
part now at <0, 0.5, -3>
part now at <0, 1.3, -3>
part now at <1, 0.5, -3>

Still 0.8 above: the box's middle is at y = 0.5 and the sphere at y = 1.3, both unchanged. Only z changed, from 0 to -3, on all three parts, because the vector added had zeros for x and y.

That is the reason for the list. One loop, one vector, and the car is somewhere else in one piece.

Chunk 6 of 6

The arrow keys move the whole model

This is session 1's held-key loop with the list from chunk 5 inside it. The four gates do not move anything themselves. They set move, and the loop over the parts adds move to every part. When no key is held, move stays vec(0, 0, 0).

from vpython import *

scene.caption = "Click the scene, then hold an arrow key."
ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(20, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)
parts = []
parts.append(box(pos=vec(0, 0.5, 0), size=vec(2, 1, 1), color=color.blue))
parts.append(sphere(pos=vec(0, 1.3, 0), radius=0.4, color=color.yellow))
parts.append(cone(pos=vec(1, 0.5, 0), axis=vec(1, 0, 0), radius=0.5, color=color.red))
speed = 0.2

while True:
    rate(30)
    keys = keysdown()

    move = vec(0, 0, 0)
    if "right" in keys:
        move = vec(speed, 0, 0)
    if "left" in keys:
        move = vec(-speed, 0, 0)
    if "up" in keys:
        move = vec(0, 0, -speed)
    if "down" in keys:
        move = vec(0, 0, speed)

    for part in parts:
        part.pos = part.pos + move

    if len(keys) > 0:
        print(keys, " nose is at", parts[2].pos + parts[2].axis)

Predict: On a pass where no key is held, what does move hold, and what does the loop over the parts do? Then: the right arrow is held for three passes. Where is the nose of the cone afterwards?

What it actually prints (run without a browser, quit after 8 passes of the loop)

['right']  nose is at <2.2, 0.5, 0>
['right']  nose is at <2.4, 0.5, 0>
['right']  nose is at <2.6, 0.5, 0>
['down']  nose is at <2.6, 0.5, 0.2>

Fed in by the build script: the right arrow key held on passes 2 to 4, the down arrow key held on pass 6.

With no key held, move is vec(0, 0, 0), and the loop adds nothing to each part, so the car stays where it is. The loop runs every pass whether a key is held or not.

After three passes of the right arrow the nose is at <2.6, 0.5, 0>: it started at x = 2 (the cone's pos at 1 plus its axis of 1) and moved 0.2 three times. The down arrow then adds 0.2 to z.

A house whose size is one number

The body is a box, so its pos is its middle, and it has to be lifted by half its height to stand on the ground. The roof is a pyramid pointing up: its pos is the middle of its base, which is the top face of the body, and its axis points along y.

from vpython import *

ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(20, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)

def make_house(x, z, size):
    body = box(pos=vec(x, size / 2, z), size=vec(size, size, size), color=color.white)
    roof = pyramid(pos=vec(x, size, z), size=vec(size / 2, size, size), axis=vec(0, 1, 0), color=color.red)
    print("house of size", size, " body bottom at y =", body.pos.y - body.size.y / 2, " roof point at y =", roof.pos.y + roof.size.x)

make_house(-5, 0, 1)
make_house(0, 0, 2)
make_house(6, 0, 3)

Predict: Three houses of size 1, 2 and 3. Where is the bottom of each body? And how high is the point of the size 3 roof compared with the size 1 roof?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

house of size 1  body bottom at y = 0.0  roof point at y = 1.5
house of size 2  body bottom at y = 0.0  roof point at y = 3.0
house of size 3  body bottom at y = 0.0  roof point at y = 4.5

Every body's bottom is at y = 0.0, because each pos is lifted by exactly half its own height. The size 3 roof's point is at y = 4.5 and the size 1 roof's at y = 1.5: three times as high, because every number comes from size.

The roof's size.x is its length along the axis, which points up here, so roof.pos.y + roof.size.x is where the point is.

Running this in VS Code

Once, before the first run

Open the terminal in VS Code and install the library: pip install vpython. Check it worked with python -c "import vpython". It should print nothing at all and give you the prompt back. A traceback here is an install problem, not a problem with your program.

Every run after that

Save your file as YourName_vpython02.py: your first name, an underscore, then vpython02. Run it from the terminal. The 3D scene opens in a new browser tab, sometimes behind VS Code, so look for it before deciding that nothing happened. Click inside the scene before pressing any key. To end the program, close the tab or press Ctrl+C in the terminal. The program is written to run until you do. Run the file again and you get a fresh tab.

VPython draws solid shapes that you can turn and zoom. Everything you print still goes to the terminal, and everything you draw goes to the browser tab. Words in the terminal, pictures in the tab.
