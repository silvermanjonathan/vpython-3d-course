A car with wheels that roll along a road

A wheel is a cylinder on its side, one speed moves every part, and the wheels turn by exactly as far as the car travels

Length 90 minutes Ages 10 to 11 New 7 items

What is new today

a wheel sits on its radius, two lists for one model: parts and rolling, a road built by a loop, speed in units per second with dt, rolling: 360 degrees per circumference, a negative angle rolls backwards, a loop-back gate that shifts every part.

Deliberately re-used: Session 2: a function that makes objects, a model in a list, cylinder and axis. Session 3: dt and a speed. Session 4: rotate with radians, a degrees counter, the camera that follows.

The whole session is the car from session 4 given wheels. The one new idea is in chunk 5: how far a wheel should turn is worked out from how far the car moved, not picked. Everything before chunk 5 is setup for it, and the hinge question checks that the room got it. Keep the setup brisk.

Opener · 10 minutes

Retrieval: turning, the axis, and the loop

Paper first. Three questions from the car you drove in session 4.

car.rotate(angle=radians(30), axis=vec(0, 1, 0))

Question: What does radians(30) mean in this line, and what goes wrong if the radians is left off?

It is how you write 30 degrees to VPython. Without it the 30 means something much bigger, nearly five full turns, and the car spins wildly on every tap.

Question: A cylinder is made with pos=vec(2, 0, 0) and axis=vec(0, 0, 3). Where is its other end? Is its pos in the middle of it?

The other end is at <2, 0, 3>, pos plus axis. No: for a cylinder, pos is one flat end, not the middle. A box and a sphere have their pos in the middle, a cylinder does not.

Question: The car's three parts were kept in a list and moved by one loop. Why not three separate move lines?

Three lines would need changing every time a part was added. The loop does the same thing to every part, however many there are, and the distances between the parts cannot drift, because each part gets the same vector.

Chunk 1 of 6

A wheel is a cylinder lying on its side

A wheel is a cylinder whose axis points along z, so its flat faces look left and right and its round side touches the ground. pos is one flat face, from session 2, and pos + axis is the other. The wheel here is 0.3 thick, from z = 0.7 to z = 1.0, which is where the right side of a car 1.4 wide would be.

How high to put it is the session 1 question again, with a different answer. A box 1 tall is lifted by 0.5, half its height. A wheel is lifted by its radius: the lowest point of the round side is the radius below the middle. Radius 0.4 means pos.y = 0.4, and then the wheel just touches a ground whose top is at y = 0.

from vpython import *

ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(20, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)
wheel = cylinder(pos=vec(1, 0.4, 0.7), axis=vec(0, 0, 0.3), radius=0.4, color=color.black)
print("one face is at", wheel.pos)
print("the other face is at", wheel.pos + wheel.axis)
print("the lowest point of the wheel is at y =", wheel.pos.y - wheel.radius)

Predict: The wheel's pos is <1, 0.4, 0.7>. Where is its other flat face? And where is the lowest point of the wheel: on the ground, under it, or floating above it?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

one face is at <1, 0.4, 0.7>
the other face is at <1, 0.4, 1>
the lowest point of the wheel is at y = 0.0

The other face is at <1, 0.4, 1>: pos plus the axis, which adds 0.3 to z and nothing else.

The lowest point is at y = 0.0, exactly on the ground. The middle of the wheel is 0.4 up and the round side reaches 0.4 below it, the radius. Change the radius to 0.6 and leave pos alone, and the wheel sinks 0.2 into the ground. For a wheel, the lift is the radius.

Chunk 2 of 6

Four wheels from one function, into two lists

add_wheel(x, z) is session 2's tree function with a different job: a black cylinder and a white spoke at the place you give it. The spoke is a thin box across the middle of the wheel, so that you can see the wheel turn. A plain black cylinder looks the same whether it is turning or not.

The function has no return line. It puts what it makes into lists. Every part goes in parts, the list of everything that moves with the car. The spoke also goes in rolling, the list of everything that turns when the car rolls. One object can be in two lists at once.

from vpython import *

ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(20, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)
body = box(pos=vec(0, 0.9, 0), size=vec(3, 0.8, 1.4), color=color.red)
parts = [body]
rolling = []

def add_wheel(x, z):
    wheel = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z), axis=vec(0, 0, 0.3), radius=0.4, color=color.black)
    spoke = box(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z + 0.15), size=vec(0.7, 0.08, 0.32), color=color.white)
    parts.append(wheel)
    parts.append(spoke)
    rolling.append(spoke)

add_wheel(1, 0.7)
add_wheel(-1, 0.7)
add_wheel(1, -1)
add_wheel(-1, -1)
print(len(parts), "parts move with the car")
print(len(rolling), "of them turn when it rolls")
print("the first spoke is at", rolling[0].pos)

Predict: The function is called four times. How many objects end up in parts, counting the body? How many in rolling? And is the first spoke inside its wheel, or beside it?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

9 parts move with the car
4 of them turn when it rolls
the first spoke is at <1, 0.4, 0.85>

Nine in parts: the body, plus a wheel and a spoke from each of the four calls. Four in rolling: only the spokes.

The first spoke is inside its wheel, at <1, 0.4, 0.85>. Its z is the wheel's z plus 0.15, which is halfway along the wheel's axis of 0.3, so the spoke's middle is the wheel's middle. Its size is 0.32 deep, a little more than the wheel is thick, so the white shows at both faces. The wheels on the left have z = -1, and their faces run from -1 to -0.7, against the left side of the body.

Chunk 3 of 6

A road made by a loop

The road is one long, thin grey box with its top at y = 0, so the wheels from chunk 1 sit on it. The dashes down the middle are ten small yellow boxes, placed by a loop. Each one is 4 further along than the one before, starting at x = -18. A dash is 0.02 tall and sits at y = 0.01, so it lies on the road instead of inside it.

A counter in the loop checks that the loop ran the number of times you meant, the way session 4's degrees counter checked a spin.

from vpython import *

grass = box(pos=vec(0, -0.2, 0), size=vec(60, 0.2, 30), color=color.green)
road = box(pos=vec(0, -0.05, 0), size=vec(40, 0.1, 4), color=color.gray(0.4))
dashes = 0

for i in range(10):
    dash = box(pos=vec(-18 + i * 4, 0.01, 0), size=vec(1.5, 0.02, 0.2), color=color.yellow)
    dashes = dashes + 1

print(dashes, "dashes, the last one at x =", dash.pos.x)
print("the road runs from x =", road.pos.x - road.size.x / 2, "to x =", road.pos.x + road.size.x / 2)

Predict: The first dash is at x = -18 and each is 4 further on. Where is the tenth dash? Is it still on the road, which is 40 long with its middle at 0?

What it actually prints (run without a browser)

10 dashes, the last one at x = 18.0
the road runs from x = -20.0 to x = 20.0

The tenth dash is at x = 18: the loop's i runs from 0 to 9, and -18 plus 9 times 4 is 18. The road runs from x = -20 to x = 20, so the last dash is 2 short of the end, with the same gap at the other end.

After the loop, dash holds the last box it made, which is why the print line can ask it for its pos. The other nine dashes are in the world but no name points at them, and that is fine, because nothing in this program moves a dash.

Chunk 4 of 6

One speed moves every part

Session 3 moved a ball by its velocity times dt, where dt is the slice of a second that one pass takes. The car does the same. speed is 3, in units per second, and step is how far one pass moves it: 3 times 1/30, which is 0.1.

The loop over parts adds the same vec(step, 0, 0) to every part, so the wheels stay where they are on the body. parts[1] is the first wheel, 1 ahead of the body, and the print lines check that it stays 1 ahead.

from vpython import *

ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(60, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)
body = box(pos=vec(0, 0.9, 0), size=vec(3, 0.8, 1.4), color=color.red)
parts = [body]

def add_wheel(x, z):
    wheel = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z), axis=vec(0, 0, 0.3), radius=0.4, color=color.black)
    parts.append(wheel)

add_wheel(1, 0.7)
add_wheel(-1, 0.7)
add_wheel(1, -1)
add_wheel(-1, -1)

speed = 3
dt = 1 / 30
passes = 0

while True:
    rate(30)
    passes = passes + 1
    step = speed * dt
    for part in parts:
        part.pos = part.pos + vec(step, 0, 0)

    if passes == 30:
        print("after 1 second the body is at x =", round(body.pos.x, 2), " front wheel at x =", round(parts[1].pos.x, 2))
    if passes == 60:
        print("after 2 seconds the body is at x =", round(body.pos.x, 2), " front wheel at x =", round(parts[1].pos.x, 2))

Predict: The speed is 3 units per second. Where is the body after one second, and after two? Is the front wheel still exactly 1 ahead of the body on both lines?

What it actually prints (run without a browser, quit after 60 passes of the loop)

after 1 second the body is at x = 3.0  front wheel at x = 4.0
after 2 seconds the body is at x = 6.0  front wheel at x = 7.0

After one second the body is at x = 3.0 and after two it is at x = 6.0: thirty passes of 0.1 each, then thirty more. The front wheel is at 4.0 and then 7.0, still 1 ahead on both lines, because every part got the same step on every pass.

Change speed to 6 and the body is at 6.0 after one second with nothing else touched. That is why the speed is a number of units per second and not a number per pass: you can read it, and rate can change without the car changing speed.

Chunk 5 of 6

The wheels roll: one circumference is one full turn

A wheel that slides along without turning looks wrong at once. It should turn by exactly as much as it travels. A wheel that rolls one full turn, 360 degrees, has travelled once around its own rim: its circumference, which is 2 * pi * radius. So the degrees for one pass are 360 times the step, divided by the circumference.

The car rolls to the right, and a wheel rolling to the right turns clockwise, which is a negative angle around z, the way session 4 turned the car right with radians(-30). The spokes are in rolling, and each one rotates around a line along z through its own pos, which is the middle of its wheel. A turned counter adds up the degrees.

The wheel's radius is 0.4, so its circumference is 2.51. The car moves 0.1 per pass.

from vpython import *

ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(60, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)
body = box(pos=vec(0, 0.9, 0), size=vec(3, 0.8, 1.4), color=color.red)
parts = [body]
rolling = []

def add_wheel(x, z):
    wheel = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z), axis=vec(0, 0, 0.3), radius=0.4, color=color.black)
    spoke = box(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z + 0.15), size=vec(0.7, 0.08, 0.32), color=color.white)
    parts.append(wheel)
    parts.append(spoke)
    rolling.append(spoke)

add_wheel(1, 0.7)
add_wheel(-1, 0.7)
add_wheel(1, -1)
add_wheel(-1, -1)

radius = 0.4
speed = 3
dt = 1 / 30
moved = 0
turned = 0
passes = 0
print("circumference of a wheel:", round(2 * pi * radius, 2))

while True:
    rate(30)
    passes = passes + 1
    step = speed * dt
    for part in parts:
        part.pos = part.pos + vec(step, 0, 0)
    moved = moved + step

    turn = 360 * step / (2 * pi * radius)
    for spoke in rolling:
        spoke.rotate(angle=radians(-turn), axis=vec(0, 0, 1))
    turned = turned + turn

    if passes == 25:
        print("pass 25: moved", round(moved, 2), " turned", round(turned, 1), "degrees")
    if passes == 26:
        print("pass 26: moved", round(moved, 2), " turned", round(turned, 1), "degrees")
    if passes == 30:
        print("pass 30: moved", round(moved, 2), " turned", round(turned, 1), "degrees, which is",
              round(turned / 360, 2), "full turns")

Predict: The circumference is 2.51 and the car moves 0.1 per pass. On which pass does turned go past 360? After one second the car has moved 3: is that more or less than one full turn of the wheel?

What it actually prints (run without a browser, quit after 30 passes of the loop)

circumference of a wheel: 2.51
pass 25: moved 2.5  turned 358.1 degrees
pass 26: moved 2.6  turned 372.4 degrees
pass 30: moved 3.0  turned 429.7 degrees, which is 1.19 full turns

On pass 26. After 25 passes the car has moved 2.5, just short of one circumference, and turned is 358.1. One more pass takes it to 2.6 and 372.4 degrees. The spoke has gone once round and a little more.

More than one full turn: 3 is bigger than 2.51, and the output box shows 429.7 degrees, which is 1.19 turns. A bigger wheel would turn less for the same trip: with radius 0.8 the circumference doubles and the same 3 units is only 0.6 of a turn. The turn line works that out from radius, so changing the wheel changes the turn with it.

Chunk 6 of 6

Off the end of the road, and back to the start

The road is 40 long, so the car reaches its end. A gate on the body's x sends the car back to the other end once the body is past x = 21, which is when the back wheel has left the road. The gate must not put the body back on its own, because the wheels would then be 42 units behind it. Every part gets the same move, as always: subtract vec(42, 0, 0) from all five, and the car is off the left end of the road in one piece, ready to drive on again.

The speed is 4 here so that the trip takes less time. Nothing else about the moving changes.

Session 4's camera lines are here too. scene.camera.follow(body) keeps the car in view the whole way, and the road slides past under it.

from vpython import *

grass = box(pos=vec(0, -0.2, 0), size=vec(60, 0.2, 30), color=color.green)
road = box(pos=vec(0, -0.05, 0), size=vec(40, 0.1, 4), color=color.gray(0.4))
body = box(pos=vec(0, 0.9, 0), size=vec(3, 0.8, 1.4), color=color.red)
parts = [body]

def add_wheel(x, z):
    wheel = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z), axis=vec(0, 0, 0.3), radius=0.4, color=color.black)
    parts.append(wheel)

add_wheel(1, 0.7)
add_wheel(-1, 0.7)
add_wheel(1, -1)
add_wheel(-1, -1)

scene.autoscale = False
scene.range = 8
scene.forward = vec(0, -0.5, -1)
scene.camera.follow(body)

speed = 4
dt = 1 / 30
passes = 0

while True:
    rate(30)
    passes = passes + 1
    step = speed * dt
    for part in parts:
        part.pos = part.pos + vec(step, 0, 0)

    if passes == 157:
        print("pass 157: body at x =", round(body.pos.x, 2))
    if body.pos.x > 21:
        for part in parts:
            part.pos = part.pos - vec(42, 0, 0)
        print("back to the other end on pass", passes, " body at x =", round(body.pos.x, 2),
              " front wheel at x =", round(parts[1].pos.x, 2))

Predict: The body starts at x = 0 and speed 4 moves it 0.13 per pass, a little more. About how many passes until the body is past 21? Right after the gate fires, is the front wheel still 1 ahead of the body?

What it actually prints (run without a browser, quit after 160 passes of the loop)

pass 157: body at x = 20.93
back to the other end on pass 158  body at x = -20.93  front wheel at x = -19.93

About 158 passes: 21 divided by 0.13 is a little under 158. After 157 passes the body is at 20.93, not yet past 21, so the gate waits. On pass 158 it is at 21.07, the gate fires, and 42 is taken off, which puts the body at x = -20.93 and the front wheel at -19.93, still 1 ahead.

In the tab the car vanishes past the right end of the road and comes on at the left end, with the camera jumping to it. If the gate had done body.pos = vec(0, 0.9, 0) instead, the body would jump and the wheels would carry on without it, so the loop over the parts is the whole point of the gate.

Hinge question · from today's chunks

Before you build

Question: Two students each write the wheel line as spoke.rotate(angle=radians(5), axis=vec(0, 0, 1)), 5 degrees on every pass. One car has speed = 6. The other has speed = 1. Describe what each car's wheels look like, and say what the one fix is for both.

The fast car's wheels turn far too slowly for how far it travels: they look like they are skidding along the road. The slow car's wheels turn far too fast for its crawl: they look like they are spinning on ice. A fixed 5 degrees cannot be right for both, because the right turn depends on the step.

The fix is the chunk 5 line: turn = 360 * step / (2 * pi * radius), then radians(-turn). At speed 6 that is 28.6 degrees per pass, and at speed 1 it is 4.8. Whatever the speed, the wheel turns by exactly as far as the car moves.

Speed on the keys, and wheels that roll backwards

The speed starts at 0 and the keys change it a little on every pass they are held, so holding up is a smooth start and not a jump. Two gates keep it between -3 and 6. A negative speed makes a negative step, and a negative step makes a negative turn, so radians(-turn) comes out positive and the spokes turn the other way. Nothing in the rolling lines knows which way the car is going. The sign does the work.

from vpython import *

scene.caption = "Click the scene. Up speeds the car up, down slows it and then reverses it."
ground = box(pos=vec(0, -0.1, 0), size=vec(60, 0.2, 20), color=color.green)
body = box(pos=vec(0, 0.9, 0), size=vec(3, 0.8, 1.4), color=color.red)
parts = [body]
rolling = []

def add_wheel(x, z):
    wheel = cylinder(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z), axis=vec(0, 0, 0.3), radius=0.4, color=color.black)
    spoke = box(pos=vec(x, 0.4, z + 0.15), size=vec(0.7, 0.08, 0.32), color=color.white)
    parts.append(wheel)
    parts.append(spoke)
    rolling.append(spoke)

add_wheel(1, 0.7)
add_wheel(-1, 0.7)
add_wheel(1, -1)
add_wheel(-1, -1)

radius = 0.4
speed = 0
dt = 1 / 30

while True:
    rate(30)
    keys = keysdown()
    if "up" in keys:
        speed = speed + 0.2
    if "down" in keys:
        speed = speed - 0.2
    if speed > 6:
        speed = 6
    if speed < -3:
        speed = -3

    step = speed * dt
    for part in parts:
        part.pos = part.pos + vec(step, 0, 0)

    turn = 360 * step / (2 * pi * radius)
    for spoke in rolling:
        spoke.rotate(angle=radians(-turn), axis=vec(0, 0, 1))

    if len(keys) > 0:
        print(keys, " speed", round(speed, 1), " turn this pass", round(turn, 2), "degrees")

Predict: Up is held for three passes, then down for five. What is the speed after the three ups, and after the five downs? Does the sign of turn change, and what does that look like on the spokes?

The 3D scene opens in a new browser tab, sometimes behind VS Code, so look for it before deciding that nothing happened. Click inside the scene before pressing any key. To end the program, close the tab or press Ctrl+C in the terminal. The program is written to run until you do. Run the file again and you get a fresh tab.

VPython draws solid shapes that you can turn and zoom. Everything you print still goes to the terminal, and everything you draw goes to the browser tab. Words in the terminal, pictures in the tab.
